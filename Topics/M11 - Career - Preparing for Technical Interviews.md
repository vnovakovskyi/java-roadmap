# Preparing for Technical Interviews

> [!info] Topic
> **Area:** Career · **Module:** 11 · **Priority:** must · **Est:** 6h
> **Why this matters:** Junior interviews test fundamentals, how you think out loud, and whether you
> can explain your own projects. Every Self-check in this vault is interview practice — this topic
> turns them into a plan.

## Learning Objectives
- Know the typical stages of a junior Java interview.
- Revise the most-asked Java, Spring, SQL, and testing topics.
- Answer behavioral questions with clear stories.

## Learn
- [ ] Read:
      [Tech Interview Handbook — Software Engineering Interview Guide](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/software-engineering-interview-guide/)
      and [Behavioral Interview](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/).
- [ ] Practice with: [roadmap.sh — Java questions](https://roadmap.sh/questions/java).
- [ ] Your own question bank: the Self-check sections of every topic in this vault.

## Concepts
- [ ] Typical process: recruiter screen → technical interview (Java core, OOP, collections, Spring,
      SQL, Git, testing) → live coding or take-home → behavioral/team fit → offer.
- [ ] Most-asked junior topics: `equals`/`hashCode` and how `HashMap` works, `String` immutability,
      checked vs unchecked exceptions, interfaces vs abstract classes, collections and Big-O,
      streams and lambdas, SOLID, IoC/DI, `@Transactional`, REST methods and status codes, SQL
      joins, indexes and transactions, the test pyramid.
- [ ] Talk through your projects: the problem, your design decisions, trade-offs, what you'd do
      differently.
- [ ] Behavioral answers with **STAR**: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- [ ] When you don't know: say so, reason out loud, and describe how you'd find out — that's a good
      answer.
- [ ] Mock interviews (with a peer, or an AI acting as interviewer) make the real one feel familiar.

## Practice
**Goal:** Walk into interviews prepared.
**Steps:**
- [ ] Go through the Self-check questions of Modules 2–9, mark the ones you couldn't answer, and
      revisit those topics.
- [ ] Prepare a 2-minute pitch for each portfolio project, including one trade-off you made.
- [ ] Write five STAR stories (a bug you solved, a conflict, a failure, learning something fast,
      teamwork).
- [ ] Do two mock interviews and prepare three questions to ask the employer.

## Self-check
Answer out loud or on paper first, then expand to compare.

> [!question]- How should you answer a question you don't know?
> Admit it honestly, reason about it from what you do know, and explain how you'd find the answer —
> interviewers value the thinking more than a guess.

> [!question]- What does STAR stand for?
> Situation, Task, Action, Result — a structure for telling a concise, concrete behavioral story.

> [!question]- What are interviewers evaluating in a live-coding exercise besides the solution?
> How you clarify the problem, communicate your approach, handle hints and edge cases, test your
> code, and reason about complexity.

## Definition of Done
- [ ] I can answer the Self-check questions of Modules 2–9.
- [ ] I have project pitches and STAR stories ready.
- [ ] I've done at least two mock interviews.
- [ ] I can answer the Self-check questions without peeking.
